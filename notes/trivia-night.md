# Trivia night for 20 to 50 people (Bangalore bar or club)

A good night like this mostly comes down to two things: everyone answers every question, and the questions can be worked out rather than just remembered. Below are three possible settings, one recommended rule set, a token plan for Chickpet, and where to learn more.

## First, a format choice that matters a lot

There are two quiz traditions, and with 20 to 50 people you need to pick carefully:

- **Western pub quiz:** every team writes answers on a sheet at the same time, and you mark them after each round. **This handles 12+ teams easily, and nobody sits idle.**
- **Indian circuit quiz** (what you'd see at college fests and KQA). Questions go to one team at a time:
  - **Bounce:** a missed question passes to the next team.
  - **Pounce:** any team can secretly answer out of turn, at +10 if right and -5 if wrong.

  It's very dramatic, but with 12 teams, 11 of them are just watching.

**Use written rounds for the main part of the night, then put the top 3 or 4 teams on stage for a pounce-and-bounce final.** That way everyone stays involved for most of the night and you still get a dramatic finish.

## Team sizing

| Registrations | Team size | Teams |
|---|---|---|
| ~20 | 3 to 4 | 5 to 7 |
| ~30 | 4 | 7 to 8 |
| ~50 | 4 | 12 to 13 |

- **Cap teams at 4 and avoid pairs** unless turnout is low. Pairs have less to discuss, and the discussion is the fun part.
- **Let people register solo, in pairs or as full teams**, then merge the solos and pairs into teams yourself. Strangers sharing a table is a big part of what makes a communal night work.
- **Keep it to about 6 to 14 teams.** Fewer than 6 feels thin, and more than 14 makes marking too slow.

## Three settings to choose from

### A. "Board Exam Night" (the CBSE theme taken all the way)

Run the whole evening as a CBSE board exam played for laughs:

- The registration confirmation is an **admit card**, teams get **roll numbers**, answer sheets are printed like answer booklets, the host is the **invigilator**, and the break is **recess**.
- **Round formats are real CBSE question types:**
  - **Section A:** 1-mark MCQs.
  - **Match the Following.**
  - **Assertion and Reason.** Example: *A: Ice floats on water. R: Ice is denser than water.* Answer: A is true, R is false.
  - **"Draw a neat, labelled diagram"** (5 marks): a masala dosa, or the Namma Metro map from memory. Judged for laughs.
- **Results and awards:** the winners get a **"Topper" certificate** signed by "The Principal", and last place gets a fond **"Compartment"** award.
- **Why it suits you:** it's the most memorable option, and people will talk about the theme afterwards. It works best for a 25 to 35 crowd that grew up with NCERT.

### B. Classic Bangalore pub quiz (mixed topics)

**Six rounds of 8 to 10 questions** plus a final:

1. Warm-up (easy, to build confidence)
2. Namma Bengaluru / Karnataka
3. CBSE / NCERT nostalgia
4. Audio: the Doordarshan signature tune, old ad jingles (Nirma, Vicco, Liril), 90s TV intros
5. Science "explain why"
6. Wildcard / pop culture
7. Final

**Why it suits you:** it's the easiest to run the first time.

### C. "Quiz Mela" stations (better for a club)

Clubs are loud and people stand, so a seated quiz struggles there. Instead:

- **Set up 5 or 6 stations** that teams rotate through on a timer: picture wall, audio booth, estimation jar, a connecting wall, a physical task like building the tallest coaster tower, and a mascot ID board (Amul girl, Nirma girl, Gattu).
- **Each station gets a volunteer with a scoresheet.**
- **Why it suits you:** it needs less of one host's voice and more helpers.

## Rule set (works with A or B)

1. **Picture round handout at the start.** Teams work on it all night and hand it in at the break. It keeps tables busy while you mark.
2. **Joker:** each team plays a joker on one round *before* it starts, which doubles that round's points. It adds strategy and lets teams come back.
3. **Phones face down.** Cheating is an honour-system matter. In Board Exam mode, call it "caught copying" and dock that round.
4. **Half marks** for partly right answers, at the quizmaster's discretion.
5. **Teams swap sheets for marking** while you read out the answers, which is much faster than marking 12 sheets yourself. Have someone update the scoreboard on screen or a whiteboard after every round. A visible scoreboard is where the tension comes from.
6. **Final wager:** before the last question, teams bet up to half their score. It's the Jeopardy final, and any team can still win.
7. **Pounce-and-bounce stage final** for the top 3 or 4 teams, if you want a finish to watch.
8. **Tiebreaker:** an estimation question, closest answer wins. Example: "How many idlis does [a famous Bangalore darshini] sell on a Sunday?" Andaza's log-error scoring fits this perfectly.

**Side awards:** Best Team Name, Best Wrong Answer (read the funniest ones aloud, which is often the biggest laugh of the night), and a wooden spoon for last place.

## How to write the questions

**Make questions workable, not just recall.** A good question puts the answer within reach of clues, so teams get an "aha" when they solve it. For example:

> *This sweet was created in a palace kitchen. Its name joins the city to "paka", Kannada for sugar syrup. Name it.* (Mysore Pak)

**Aim for the average team to score 50 to 60%.**

- Every question should be answered by at least one team.
- No team should score zero on a round.
- If nobody gets a question, that's the question's fault, not the teams'.

**Make later rounds lighter**, with more audio, visuals and laughs, because attention drops as the drinks go down.

## Running the night

- **Pacing:** 6 rounds is about 2 to 2.5 hours including breaks. Doors open 30 minutes early to register teams and merge solo players.
- **People:** one quizmaster, one scorer and one runner. Don't run it alone.
- **Equipment:** a mic (essential in a bar), a projector, printed sheets, pens and a bell.
- **Pitching the venue:** bars want crowds on slow weeknights (Tuesday or Wednesday). Offer them a full room in exchange for the space and a free pitcher for the winners.
- **Registration:** a Google Form asking for name, team or solo, and preferred team name. You can also add a fun survey question, like "Most overrated Bangalore café?", and use the answers in a "Survey Says" round about the people in the room.

## Tokens from Chickpet

**When to go:** the Sunday Bazaar runs along Avenue Road, JC Road and KR Road, **Sundays roughly 6am to 2pm, best around 7 to 9:30am**. Take the metro to KR Market. The usual advice is to open at about 30 to 40% of the first price quoted.

**What to look for:**

- **Old coins** (pre-2011 25-paisa coins, old 1-rupee coins), glued onto a card that says "Topper, Edition 1"
- **Cassettes** with the label rewritten with the team's name
- **Amar Chitra Katha / Tinkle** comics from the Avenue Road second-hand bookshops, which suit the CBSE theme well
- **Lattoo tops and kanche marbles**
- **Brass bells**

**Rolling trophy:** buy one odd brass object, like a bell or a lota, that the winning team holds until the next edition, with team names tied onto it. If you plan to run this more than once, that ongoing story will matter more than the prize itself.

## Where to learn

**Attend in Bangalore (the best way to learn):**

- **[KQA, the Karnataka Quiz Association](https://kqaquizzes.org/):** running since 1983, about 60 open quizzes a year with cheap entry, and its big event, ASKQANCE, is every June. Go to two of them, watch how the quizmaster paces the room, and talk to them afterwards.
- **Calm Your Wits Quizzing:** a Bangalore collective that runs pub quizzes (Instagram @calm.your.wits.quizzing, past events like the [Namma Bengaluru Quiz](https://www.district.in/events/namma-bengaluru-quiz-by-calm-your-wits-quizzing-nov1-2025-buy-tickets)). This is the closest match to what you're building.
- **Event listings:** search "quiz" on District or allevents, and look at college fest quiz events (RVCE runs a large one).

**Watch (for format ideas):**

- **Only Connect** (BBC): the Connecting Wall is the best round format around. Copy it.
- **University Challenge:** the starter/bonus structure.
- **QI:** a model for workable, interesting questions.
- **Pointless** and **The Chase:** scoring mechanics.
- **Taskmaster:** physical challenges.
- **Bournvita Quiz Contest** (Derek O'Brien) and **Mastermind India** (Siddhartha Basu): the Indian quizzing feel, on YouTube.

**Play and find questions:**

- **PuzzGrid:** connecting walls made by users.
- **Sporcle** and **JetPunk:** themed quizzes.
- **J! Archive:** every Jeopardy! clue ever asked.
- **Open Trivia DB:** a free question API.
- **quizbowlpackets.com:** huge packet archives, though US-heavy.

**Tools:** Google Forms for registration. Kahoot or Mentimeter are fine for *one* phone round, but keep the rest on paper. Phones make people look at screens, while a shared answer sheet gets the table talking.
